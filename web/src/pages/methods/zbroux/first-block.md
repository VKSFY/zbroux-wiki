The first block is exactly the same as in Roux. It's made of:

- the bottom-left edge (DL),
- the left centre,
- two pairs, each a corner and an edge: one at the front (DFL and FL) and one at the back (DBL and BL).

When it's finished, the block sits at the bottom left and matches the left centre.

## How to build it

A simple order to start with:

1. Put the DL edge in place next to the left centre. This is usually the easiest part to see in inspection.
2. Build the first pair (a corner with its edge) and insert it next to the DL edge.
3. Build the second pair and insert it on the other side.

Once that feels comfortable, stop following a fixed order. Look for pieces that are already next to each other and build around them. That's where the short first blocks come from.

## Inspection

Try to plan the whole first block in inspection, or at least the DL edge and the first pair. If you also spot where the second block's bottom edge (DR) is, you can go straight into step 2 without stopping.

## Colour neutrality

Being able to start from more than one block makes the first block shorter on average. A common choice is x2 y neutral: either of two opposite colours on the bottom, with any of the four side colours as the left block, which gives 8 options per solve. Being fully colour neutral gives even more options, but inspection gets harder.

## Practice

- Practise the first block on its own with a block trainer: scramble, build only the block, and aim for few moves rather than speed at first.
- Anything written about the Roux first block applies here, because the step is the same.
